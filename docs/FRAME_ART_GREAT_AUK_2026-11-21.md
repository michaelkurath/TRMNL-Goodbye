# The Great Auk on TRMNL Frame

Verified 21 November 2026.

The great auk is extinct. Its last widely accepted breeding pair was killed on Eldey, Iceland, in 1844; the Natural History Museum also records a later sighting in 1852, so 1844 is a breeding-pair endpoint rather than a universally certain date for the final individual. Preserved skins, skeletons and eggs survive in museums. NHM documents its British specimen and great auk eggs. These images reconstruct a living bird in summer plumage on an imagined North Atlantic basalt shore, not an identified historical individual.
