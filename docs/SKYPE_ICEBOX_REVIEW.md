## Skype

**Decision:** approved for live catalogue as exhibit 036.

Fact review:
- Microsoft Support states that Skype consumer was retired on 5 May 2025 and
  explicitly distinguishes it from Skype for Business:
  https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/skype/f596c90a-b3b0-42c2-b120-d0630642f1f9
- Microsoft's announcement says Skype would remain available until 5 May 2025
  while Microsoft shifted consumer communications toward Teams Free:
  https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/blog/2025/02/28/the-next-chapter-moving-from-skype-to-microsoft-teams/

Text review:
- The lifespan and note use the consumer-service endpoint, not Skype for
  Business.
- The explanation is framed as Microsoft's product consolidation decision and
  avoids claiming that every Skype-related paid function vanished on the same
  date.

Image review:
- AI-generated monochrome archival engraving/halftone artwork; no third-party
  photograph is embedded.
- The composition uses period-appropriate laptop, webcam, headset, and a
  generic two-person video-call window without the Skype logo or readable UI.
- Master and standard JPEG crops are 1600×800 and 1067×800 and keep the
  identifying hardware inside the centered safe zone.
- The first flat/vector treatment was rejected on 23 September 2026 because it
  did not match GOODBYE's established museum-engraving visual language.

## Domestic icebox

**Decision:** approved for live catalogue as exhibit 037.

Fact review:
- Smithsonian National Museum of American History describes wooden, metal-lined
  household iceboxes, daily block-ice delivery, and widespread household use
  into the 1930s:
  https://americanhistory.si.edu/explore/stories/keeping-your-food-cool-ice-harvesting-electric-refrigeration
- A second Smithsonian exhibit notes that Americans increasingly replaced
  unreliable iceboxes with newly affordable electric refrigerators during the
  1930s:
  https://www.americanhistory.si.edu/explore/exhibitions/object-project/online/refrigerators

Text review:
- The endpoint remains explicitly approximate (`1930s*`) because replacement
  was gradual and regionally uneven.
- The wording describes a transition in American households rather than a
  universal worldwide disappearance.

Image review:
- AI-generated monochrome archival engraving/halftone artwork; no third-party
  photograph is embedded.
- The composition depicts a wooden insulated cabinet, upper ice compartment,
  block ice, lower storage, drain hardware, hinges, and ice tools without a
  modern compressor.
- Master and standard JPEG crops are 1600×800 and 1067×800 and keep the subject
  inside the centered safe zone.
- The first flat/vector treatment was rejected on 23 September 2026 because it
  did not match GOODBYE's established museum-engraving visual language.
